Covington Cards

Ten games, as we actually play them. Most of them were invented in this town. Arguments settled by whatever is written here.

Wild-card rules in play

Game
Family
How it goes
Five-Card Stud ♠
Stud

One card down, four up, a bet on every street. No draw, no community, nowhere to hide — you play the cards you were given and the cards everyone can already see. Oldest game in the book and still the meanest.

Seven-Card Stud ♣
Stud

Two down, four up, one down. Five betting rounds, best five of your seven. Everything the table needs to know is showing; the rest is arithmetic and nerve.

Criss Cross ♦
Community

Four in the hole and five on the table laid out in a cross. Community cards turn one at a time with betting between. At the end you play the row or the column — three cards, never the middle twice — plus two of your own.

Krogering (low hold)
Stud

Seven-card stud, except your lowest down card is wild — and so is every other card of that rank in your hand. Catch a lower one on the last card and the whole hand rearranges itself. Named for the man who would not stop dealing it.

Chase the Queen ♥
Stud

Seven-card stud. Every time a queen lands face up, the next up card dealt is wild for everybody — and a fresh queen retires the old wild. If the last queen shows on a final down card, nothing is wild and somebody is very unhappy.

Screw Your Neighbor
Lives

One card each, three lives apiece. In turn you keep what you have or trade it with the player on your left; the dealer's only out is the top of the deck. Lowest card at the end loses a life. A king in hand cannot be taken.

Liar's Poker
No cards

No deck at all. Everyone holds a dollar bill and bids on the digits in the serial numbers — three fives, four sevens — each bid beating the last. Call the bluff and lay the bills on the table. The worst liars lose the most money.

Beat Charlie
Dead hand

A hand is dealt face down to the middle of the table. That is Charlie, and Charlie does not bet, fold, or complain. Everybody plays the pot against him. Beat Charlie and you split what's there; lose to him and you leave it for the next deal.

In Between
Pot game

Two cards face up, then you bet on whether the next one lands between them. Hit either post and you pay double into the pot. Ace–deuce showing is an invitation; a five and a six is a trap.

Night Baseball
Blind

Seven cards face down and nobody looks. Turn one at a time around the table and you must beat the best board showing or keep turning until you do. Threes and nines are wild, a four buys an extra card, and the hand is over before anyone knows what happened.
